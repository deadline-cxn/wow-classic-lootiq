# LootIQ — Know What You Loot

NOTE: This addon is for wow classic era 1.15.9 only.

Ever pick up an item and wonder:

**Where did I get this?**
**What else drops it?**
**Is this thing actually worth anything?**
**Have I looted this before?**

LootIQ is a loot-tracking and intelligence addon for World of Warcraft that builds a personal database of the items you collect and, more importantly, **where they came from**.

As you play, LootIQ records your loot history and associates items with their sources, gradually building a database based on your own adventures.

## 🧠 Your Personal Loot Database

LootIQ keeps track of the things you've collected so you can look back and see what you've found instead of relying on memory.

Track items you've looted, how often you've seen them, and the sources you've encountered while playing.

The more you play, the more useful your LootIQ database becomes.

## 🗺️ Know Where Your Loot Came From

LootIQ isn't interested in only telling you that you picked up **[Extremely Questionable Sword of Questionable Decisions]**.

It wants to know where you got it.

LootIQ records source information alongside your loot, helping you build a history connecting items to the creatures, quests, and other sources you've encountered.

That means your loot history becomes useful information rather than just a giant list of stuff you once shoved into your bags.

## 💰 Auctionator Integration

If you use Auctionator, LootIQ can leverage its auction-house pricing data to give your loot some financial context.

That weird green item you nearly vendored?

Maybe it's junk.

Maybe it's worth 47 gold.

**LootIQ would prefer that you know before making terrible financial decisions.**

## ❗ Questie Integration

LootIQ can also works alongside Questie to provide additional information related to quests and item sources.

Together, LootIQ, Questie, and Auctionator help connect three important questions:

**What is it?**
**Where did it come from?**
**What's it worth?**

Instead of loot disappearing into your bags and eventually being forgotten:
